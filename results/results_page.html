Multi-agent simulation · 16 agents · 9 eras · ~45 agent runs

One startup, fifty years, one job: reinvent toward the frontier

A 16-person AI org lived through 1990–2020 as training. In each era it decided using only what was knowable then, and a historian-judge then revealed what actually happened and scored the call. From 2026 it forecast without an answer key. The interactive trajectory below shows Run 001; the separate v2 pilot is summarized after it.

Score by era

Training eras (1990–2020) were scored against real history. 2026 was scored against today's real market. 2032 and 2040 are scored against projected worlds and have no ground truth.

What the company became in each era

What 50 years of reinvention taught it

It kept naming the frontier, then building the layer next to it

Every era's memos found the real jump: the Web index in 1990, relevance in 1996, neural nets in 2008, scale in 2020. The board kept filtering by "what can our assets reach in 18 months" and landed one layer toward comfort. The result was nine companies that were useful and acquirable, and none that defined an era.

The dissent was right more often than the decision

Dissents that asked "who holds the money or the loss?" or "who will absorb us?" were right almost every time. Dissents about a technology curve were right on the science and wrong on timing.

The best idea was deferred four times

Pricing against the loss (guarantees, underwriting) was right in 2014, when Forter and Riskified won with it. The org deferred it in 2020, 2026 and 2032, took it in 2040 against eight or more rivals, and scored 58.

Value moves from recording to carrying the loss

From 2014 to 2040 the through-line held: value moved from recording what systems did to carrying the loss when they fail.

The Playbook, distilled

  1. Bridges expire when the standards war ends. Put a date on your own death on day one.
  2. The platform owner absorbs the tool: free Analytics, first-party install ads, Cloud Vision, model-owner evals.
  3. A referee is a franchise only if a third party mandates it.
  4. Money pools where intent or loss is priced, and it goes to whoever holds it.
  5. A public curve pools value at its scarce inputs: chips, labs, expert data.
  6. Sell on the credit calendar, not the product calendar. These were the best moves of the run.
  7. Date your option against the incumbent's ship date.
  8. Voluntary intent builds data moats; hub-and-spoke networks don't.
  9. Last era's road not taken is this era's crowded trade.
  10. Stacked speculations multiply. Fund the fallback as the base case.

Limits of this simulation

  • All 16 agents and both judges are the same language model, and it knows what happened in 1990–2020. Hindsight leakage was scored every era and was worst in 2008 (5/10) and 2020 (4/10).
  • The 2020 board meeting was written by the orchestrator while agent launches were down, so it had no independent Red Team.
  • The forecast eras are circular: the 2032 kill case became the 2040 world, and the 2040 board adopted it.
  • Revenues and exits are simulated distributions, not data.
