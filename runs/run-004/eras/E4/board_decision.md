# E4 board decision — 1 January 2008

**Company:** Switchyard Commerce Operations — a narrow, permissioned exception workflow for online merchants.

**Decision:** Test the merchant exception workbench through manual operation, not hosted software. For one exception category, record case mix, time, corrections, authority, and outcome. Seek a buyer-paid pilot. The purpose is to learn whether the same workflow repeats and produces net labor savings; no software margin is claimed.

**Thesis:** Web commerce produces operational exceptions, but value depends on getting an authorized person to resolve them. The bottleneck is reliable case context and decision authority. A structured workbench might own the workflow record and later provide standard intake; only shared categories across customers justify connectors or hosted automation.

**Three-year plan:** Finish a 100-case retrospective triage; then time-box a supervised paid pilot with explicit permissions and action limits. Proceed to a minimal workbench only if net handling time falls at least 20%, costly misses do not rise, and setup/maintenance remain within the red-team caps. Test a second buyer with no more than 20% customization.

**Kill criteria:** No paying authority, no permissioned cases, excessive misses, no net time reduction, setup over two person-days, maintenance over two hours/week, or non-repeatability kills the product thesis.

**Kill / keep:** Kill broad exception handling and all connectors for now. Keep manual case records, permission boundaries, and measured labor as learning assets. Maintain the support playbook only as archived know-how.

**Dissent:** CTO supports a local packet generator before hosting; CFO argues even the paid service must cap hours. CEO agrees and authorizes no build until the gates pass.
