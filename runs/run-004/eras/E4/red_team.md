# Independent Red Team critique — E4 · January 2008

Suggested gates below are proposed, not evidence. Payer and authority remain unproven; savings must exceed review, correction, and configuration effort.

## Frontier — exception triage
**Kill case:** Unknown cost owner; bad classification can shift labor rather than remove it; data formats and rules may make every merchant bespoke.
**Gate:** On 100 historical cases, prioritize ≥80% correctly and miss <5% high-cost cases; in four weeks, reduce net handling time ≥20%. Require a paid-pilot buyer. **Cheap test:** Manual triage with operator validation and time tracking.

## Product — hosted workbench
**Kill case:** Hosting creates access, uptime, data, and support obligations; product may be managed labor in disguise.
**Gate:** Net handling time −20%, no material rise in costly misses, setup ≤2 person-days, maintenance ≤2 hours/week, and second buyer ≤20% customization. **Cheap test:** Produce packets manually for two weeks, measure corrections/outcomes, ask buyer to pay before hosting.

## Market — operations budget
**Kill case:** Operations may feel pain without budget authority; support may have workers but not decision rights. Count merchant and startup labor.
**Gate:** Of 10 qualified merchants, 3 confirm measurable recurring cost, identify approver, and provide sample data; require one paid pilot, verified net savings, and a repeatable second customer. **Cheap test:** Offer a priced manual pilot to operations and support leaders separately; require actual payment, not enthusiasm.

No later financial crisis is available knowledge for this decision.
