# E5 board decision — 1 January 2014

**Company:** Switchyard Evidence Operations — permissioned preparation of one class of digital commerce dispute evidence.

The board chooses a narrow, manual test of payment-dispute evidence packets for one merchant. This is distinct from broad returns, automated fraud scoring, or a platform. It captures source records, completeness checks, and human corrections; a merchant employee retains approval and submission authority. No model-generated decision or live connector is authorized.

**Thesis:** More digital transactions create cases that require evidence review. Adoption depends on secure access and a clearly authorized reviewer; the bottleneck is complete, trustworthy case evidence, not opaque prediction. Own the reviewed packet workflow and its measured error/labor record. Automate only stable, repeated steps after two paying customers validate the same process.

**Three-year plan:** Secure one paid, permissioned pilot; test 50 cases, completeness, material error, net handling time, and review cost. If gates pass, test a second paying buyer with ≤20% custom work. Only then build a minimal, access-controlled workbench.

**Kill criteria:** No paid buyer, no case access/authority, <90% complete packets, ≥5% material error, <20% net time improvement, review >15 minutes/case, variable cost >50% revenue, or excessive customization.

**Kill / keep:** Stop general exception work and any predictive assistant. Keep permission model, case taxonomy, correction trail, and full labor accounting. No new staffing before repeatability.

**Dissent:** CEO and CTO prefer evidence assembly over general triage because it has a measurable completeness outcome. Market lead notes dispute rules may vary; the board accepts only one case type. The record does not claim this is a proven market.
