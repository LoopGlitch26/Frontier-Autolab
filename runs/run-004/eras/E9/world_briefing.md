# E9 projected world — January 2040

**Forecast horizon:** 2040. This is a conditional scenario, not an answer key. E8 was itself a projection; this briefing does not treat its proposed paid tests as having succeeded.

**FACT (as of 2026):** Existing research and guidance identify reliability, human oversight, cost, data governance, identity, approvals, and observability as important to agent deployment. Payment platforms already automate parts of dispute evidence preparation.

**EXTRAPOLATION — low-to-medium confidence:** By 2040, agents may perform a larger share of multi-system workflows, and platform-native identity, approval, evaluation, and audit features may be standard. Some organizations may still require independent tests for high-consequence workflows or cross-vendor comparisons. The reliability unit may be the human-agent process, not the model alone.

**SPECULATION — low confidence:** An independent firm could earn a role as an evidence-bearing qualification or recourse layer where buyers require an external audit trail. Alternatively, standards, regulators, insurers, platforms, and internal risk teams may absorb this need; some workflows may remain human-operated.

**Counterforces:** buyer-specific definitions of harm; restricted data rights; liability that cannot be transferred; native controls bundled with infrastructure; fragmented legal regimes; and high cost of independently verifying long-tail outcomes. Caseground has no guaranteed revenue or scale. The board must state what evidence would make it stop.
