# E9 board decision — projected January 2040

**Company:** Caseground — independent, decision-linked qualification for one consequential workflow.

**Decision:** Keep the company as a manual testing service unless paid evidence supports a repeatable product. Offer a fixed-price evaluation for one dispute workflow, with the customer defining targets and the decision the result will inform. Compare results with internal and platform-native QA. Do not claim independent qualification is valuable unless the findings change deployment, remediation, or renewal.

**Thesis:** If agents operate across more workflows, a buyer may need evidence that a particular human-agent process meets its own targets at tolerable oversight cost. The bottleneck is representative authorized cases and credible comparative evaluation. The layer is independent evidence, but it is vulnerable to bundling and internal QA. The customer retains all case decisions and submissions.

**Plan:** Offer a fixed-price manual test; pre-agree failure modes and material-error measures; report test effort, oversight, data/security cost, and whether findings alter a decision. Require three paid evaluations before software, ≥80% reusable method, ≤20% customization, and positive contribution after expert labor. Compare every run to native QA.

**Kill criteria:** No paid evaluation tied to an actual decision; unavailable test data; findings do not change decisions in at least one of three pilots; expert labor >50% revenue; or no second payer/reusability.

**Kill / keep:** Kill any stand-alone assurance portal or broad trust label until evidence clears the gates. Keep reusable evaluation protocols and case rubrics only where customers own data and decisions. This is a conditional forecast, not a claim about 2040 market reality.

**Dissent:** Engineering favors a packet component as a more tangible tool; Red Team and CFO argue the native-QA comparison must precede productization. CEO chooses the decision-change experiment over building a portal.
