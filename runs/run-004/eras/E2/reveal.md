# E2 historical reveal and score — hindsight review

The Web was publicly accessible by this date and later became a major platform, but that later scale cannot retroactively validate an internal publishing business in January 1996. Windows 95 had shipped months before the cutoff. These were legitimate contemporaneous signals; neither proves Switchyard's prospective buyer economics.

The company chose a bounded support workflow and correctly made buyer validation a gate. Its technical premise could be useful, but it is vulnerable to free vendor utilities, in-house expertise, service-provider customization, and the cost of maintaining accurate cases. The market is not established by this simulation. A favorable outcome is a small specialist tool with a few repeat customers; a downside is a services business whose deployments do not generalize.

## Playbook update

1. A manual workflow prototype can test whether data is useful before an automation build.
2. Separate user, buyer, and budget authority; they may be different people.
3. Write kill thresholds before piloting and measure actual behavior, not interview enthusiasm alone.
4. Signals about a platform do not establish demand for a particular product layer.
