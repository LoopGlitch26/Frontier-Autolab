# Market & Capital memo — E2 · January 1996

**Carmen Ortiz:** Sell reduced recurring PC/LAN support work, not an abstract management platform. An internal IT support queue places user and buyer near the workflow.

**Arjun Mehta:** Pilot revenue must exceed the installation and service burden. A service provider might see direct technician productivity value, but can pull us into customization.

**Dr. Elise Laurent:** Windows 95 is a reason to review PC support practices, not evidence buyers will purchase a standalone tool. Both buyer segments remain hypotheses.

## Buyer theses

1. **Internal IT support budget:** A defined support team uses a guided troubleshooting workflow; the IT manager pays for a bounded pilot. Measure case volume, resolution time, repeats, escalations, and labor. Main risk: savings may not map to a software budget.
2. **LAN support provider budget:** A provider licenses the workflow for technicians handling a recurring class of cases. Measure resolution time, repeat visits, escalation, and actual use. Main risk: heterogeneous clients make the product bespoke and the provider captures the value.

Test one configuration and establish baselines. Seek paid pilot conversations in both segments, identify the budget owner and procurement path, then continue with the segment that pays and produces measurable improvement without material customization. This applies the Playbook's payer-first and bounded-support rules.
