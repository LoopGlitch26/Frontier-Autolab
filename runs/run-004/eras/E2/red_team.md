# Independent Red Team critique — E2 · January 1996

These kill tests are proposed decision rules, not validated market facts.

## Frontier — repeatable troubleshooting

**Kill case:** A narrow configuration may make diagnosis repeatable, but the buyer and recurring cost remain unproven. Advice can mislead when a machine differs from assumptions, and maintaining knowledge is ongoing work. Success on one setup would not establish transfer.

**Kill threshold:** Stop if fewer than 3 of 10 support managers confirm recurring incidents and a plausible budget, or if a 20-case shadow trial resolves fewer than 70% of in-scope cases correctly without expert intervention.

**Cheapest falsification:** Manually encode common cases and test with technicians on recent, de-identified tickets. Measure accuracy, time saved, and out-of-scope rate.

## Product — technician troubleshooting assistant

**Kill case:** Value depends on current content and technicians following guidance. Incident collection does not prove diagnosis quality. A single environment constrains the initial buyer set.

**Kill threshold:** Stop if a 20-case supervised trial yields under 70% correct next steps, less than 15% median handling-time improvement, or requires over 30 minutes of expert content maintenance per case family each month.

**Cheapest falsification:** Use a paper/database prototype on closed incidents; an expert checks each recommendation.

## Market — support-provider buyer thesis

**Kill case:** Channel access and provider incentives are hypotheses; the provider may not control customer software budgets, may see extra burden, or may require broad configuration coverage. The internal buyer also lacks a validated budget path.

**Kill threshold:** Stop the provider thesis if fewer than 2 of 10 interviewed providers accept a supervised pilot or none will pay after results. Kill the internal buyer thesis if fewer than 3 of 10 managers identify recurring pain and a credible budget path.

**Cheapest falsification:** Present a manual assistant and a bounded pilot offer to both segments; test case access, user, decision-maker, and price commitment.

**Hindsight check:** Assess internal Web publishing as a January 1996 hypothesis only; do not use later adoption to validate it. Likewise, proposed troubleshooting demand must be demonstrated by the buyer tests.
