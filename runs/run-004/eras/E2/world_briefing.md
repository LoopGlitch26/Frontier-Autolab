# E2 world briefing — 1 January 1996

**Information cutoff:** 1 January 1996. This is a dated briefing, not a forecast of what became dominant.

Windows 95 shipped in August 1995, adding a major new PC environment to offices already running varied hardware, operating systems, and LAN software. The Web is publicly available after CERN's 1993 release and NCSA Mosaic; this establishes a new technical possibility, not widespread enterprise use or a proven internal-publishing market. TCP/IP and Internet use are expanding, but many business-critical systems remain local and heterogeneous. NetWare and installed client-server applications remain relevant. CD-ROM remains useful for distributing large, relatively static collections.

For Switchyard, the question is whether a narrow troubleshooting workflow has an identifiable budget owner and enough repeat incidents to support software economics. A Web publishing experiment and PC/LAN support product are both technically plausible, but neither has demonstrated a repeatable buyer. The company retains its $1.5M seed-stage constraint and must avoid building custom integrations before validating willingness to pay.

Sources for dated claims: [Computer History Museum, 1995](https://www.computerhistory.org/timeline/1995/); [CERN, Short history of the Web](https://home.cern/science/computing/the-birth-of-the-web/short-history-web/). These do not establish market size or adoption rates.
