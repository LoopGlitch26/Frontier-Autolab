# E8 projected world — January 2032

**Forecast horizon:** 2032. This is a conditional base scenario, not an observed future.

**FACT (as of 2026):** Production-agent studies report bounded runs and extensive human evaluation in their samples. READY evaluates workflow reliability jointly with human oversight and cost. Enterprise guidance emphasizes governed data, identity, approvals, release controls, and telemetry. Payment vendors already offer dispute evidence automation.

**EXTRAPOLATION — medium confidence:** By 2032, many organizations may deploy agents across a larger set of business workflows, while limiting autonomy through identity policies, action scopes, and approval rules. Evaluation may become a routine release and monitoring step, increasingly embedded in cloud, workflow, and model platforms. Buyers may compare performance against their own risk targets rather than rely on general benchmarks.

**SPECULATION — low confidence:** Some high-consequence workflows may still need independent qualification across vendors, particularly when a buyer must document whether a human-agent process meets its own error and cost limits. This could create a narrow external service or software category, but platform-native evaluation and internal QA may absorb it.

**Counterforces:** data access and case rights may block outside evaluators; domain-specific definitions of error may prevent reusable tests; liability may remain with employers and vendors; platforms may bundle logs, evaluation, and approvals. Caseground begins with no proven customer or recurring budget. Treat the 2032 buyer and market as hypotheses.
