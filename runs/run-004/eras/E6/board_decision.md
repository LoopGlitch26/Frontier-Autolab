# E6 board decision — 1 January 2020

**Company:** Switchyard Evidence Operations — a human-reviewed completeness check for one payment-dispute case category.

The board chooses the narrow checker, not a general case platform or language-model assistant. It will identify missing checklist items in approved records; a named reviewer determines whether evidence is accurate and whether to submit. No autonomous decision or submission is permitted.

**Thesis:** Digital dispute work is a candidate repeatable workflow. The bottleneck is not generic language capability but case-specific completeness, trustworthy source data, and oversight cost. A permissioned checklist may reduce manual search; measure material error separately and retain human authority.

**Plan:** Apply manual checklist to 100 historical packets, blinded/independently reviewed where feasible; measure missing-item recall, false alerts, material errors, net review time, rule maintenance, and security. Require a paid pilot and second paying buyer with ≤20% customization before building a checker. Count data controls in cost.

**Kill criteria:** Fail to detect ≥95% of missing items at <5% false alerts, fail to reduce net review time ≥20%, any unauthorized access, no paid buyer/second payer, or case-specific rule burden defeats margin.

**Kill / keep:** Kill broad AI decision support and multi-category expansion. Keep the evidence taxonomy, permission boundaries, human correction trail, and audit discipline. The company has not validated a market.

**Dissent:** CTO favors rules-only rather than ML; CEO agrees. Frontier research notes model assistance could be studied later, but only in shadow mode with human review.
