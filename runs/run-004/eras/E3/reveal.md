# E3 historical reveal and score — hindsight review

Online commerce and payment services were real by 2002, but their later scale does not prove that a small merchant would buy independent exception-triage software. XML and Web service standards were emerging; integration still carried operational costs. Switchyard's manual-first choice limits technical risk and asks a commercially meaningful question. Its core risk is whether discrepancy handling is frequent and expensive enough, and whether a merchant will share records and pay for help. A plausible outcome is a narrow outsourced operations service; a weaker outcome is bespoke reconciliation work without software margins.

## Playbook update

1. A pivot can be an experiment, not a declaration that the new market is proven.
2. For exception software, identify who can authorize the next action, not only who sees the record.
3. Manual service can validate workflow and data before connectors, but does not prove software margins.
4. Require a second-customer test before calling a workflow repeatable.
