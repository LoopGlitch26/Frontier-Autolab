# Market & Capital memo — E3 · January 2002

The dot-com crash makes revenue evidence more valuable than a Web presence. E-commerce operates; XML/Web services emerge; broadband remains uneven. These are context, not proof of a Switchyard market.

## Buyer hypotheses

1. **Internal IT support:** help-desk technician uses a guided local workflow; IT manager may pay from operations/support budget if recurring cases reduce labor or escalations. Risk: savings do not map to a separate budget.
2. **Outsourced PC/LAN provider:** technicians use the same guided workflow across clients; service operations manager may pay if repeat visits fall and cases generalize. Risk: customization turns software into consulting.

For both, manually facilitate a pilot, agree in advance on resolution time, repeat rate, escalation, adoption, and support hours, and identify who can commit funds. Do not automate, add configurations, or treat Web services as evidence of demand until a buyer pays for repeatable use. This applies the Playbook's user/buyer distinction and predeclared kill gates.
