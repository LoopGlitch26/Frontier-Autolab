# Frontier Research memo — E3 · January 2002

**Yuki:** Dot-com fallout is a reason to insist on a payer, not to chase trends. **Samuel:** E-commerce operates and XML/Web services emerge, but that proves neither Switchyard's product nor a buyer. **Ines:** Uneven broadband support and order discrepancies are possible recurring workflows. **Kofi:** Start manually; capture user, budget owner, baseline, accuracy, time, and support burden; set stop rules first.

## Proposals

1. **Guided small-office broadband troubleshooting.** Walk one support provider through DSL/cable and LAN issues. Bottleneck: inconsistent configurations. Record symptoms, steps, time, resolution and escalation. Later add verified guidance only if the same workflow recurs. Stop without an identified payer or time savings.
2. **E-commerce order-discrepancy triage.** Manually help one online merchant review order/fulfillment/payment mismatches, without live-system integration. Bottleneck: inconsistent records and authority to resolve exceptions. Track discrepancy type, handoffs, time and verified outcome; later prioritize repeat types. Stop without authorized case access, budget owner, or paid pilot.

Harada and Mensah favor preserving disciplined pilot gates; Varga sees broadband as closer to Switchyard's experience; Brandt sees commerce as a new but unproven workflow. **Playbook:** manual prototype first, separate user/buyer/budget, and do not infer demand from platform signals.
