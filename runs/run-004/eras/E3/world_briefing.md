# E3 world briefing — 1 January 2002

**Information cutoff:** 1 January 2002.

The dot-com collapse has sharply reset expectations about Internet businesses and financing. Online commerce continues, but a company cannot assume that traffic or technical novelty will become durable revenue. DSL and cable broadband are expanding unevenly; dial-up remains common. XML and Web services are being discussed as ways to connect business systems, while integration remains difficult. Security, spam, payment trust, and operational reliability matter as more transactions move online. Small businesses and institutions still use local client/server systems alongside newer Web applications.

Switchyard's support assistant has not yet demonstrated repeatable willingness to pay. Its team has workflow notes, one bounded configuration, and a disciplined pilot method, but no proven proprietary data moat. The strategic question is whether to keep testing this support wedge or redirect to a new, budget-bearing workflow exposed by networked commerce. The company has limited capital and cannot fund a marketplace or broad integration platform.

This is a scenario briefing, not a claim about market size. Any buyer or adoption statement in the memos must be tested, not assumed.
