# PLAYBOOK — lessons that compound across eras
(Empty at founding. The Record appends 3–5 lessons per training era, tagged [E#].)

## [E1] Lessons from 1990–1995 (Switchyard Systems, score 55/100)
- [E1] **Bridges expire when the standards war ends.** Translation layers between incompatible systems last only as long as the fragmentation does. Date the bridge's death on day one and fund the next layer before it arrives.
- [E1] **When an open "good-enough" protocol is winning, value pools where it becomes usable and ubiquitous**: the client, the access point, the router, the index. It does not pool in translation back to legacy islands.
- [E1] **Your own memos' "next capability" line is often the real frontier.** If the org names the bigger jump and then drops it for the safer wedge, keep a funded tripwire on it. *(Revised E2: a tripwire alone is not enough. See [E2] tripwire-gate lesson. The pattern repeated.)*
- [E1] **Order of value: pipe, then traffic, then index.** Directories and indexes pay only once an open network is large. Before that, incumbents bundle them as features. *(Extended E2: the index pays once it prices intent, through paid listings. See [E2] intent lesson.)*
- [E1] **Right science and the right decade are separate bets.** Keep deep-curve options cheap, judge them on field accuracy, and don't let them set company strategy until the data and compute exist.
- [E1 note] Founding candidate heuristics tested: "Bet on the open protocol that already works" was CONFIRMED. "Own the layer that indexes, not only the pipe" was CONFIRMED but mistimed (see pipe, then traffic, then index). "Sell the outcome, never the buzzword" was plausible but untested. "Sell to budgets that already exist" is REFINED: existing budgets fund the wedge, but they also anchor you to the old architecture.

## [E2] Lessons from 1996–2001 (Manifest Networks, score 52/100)
- [E2] **Gate a tripwire on capability evidence, not on whether incumbents will pay.** "Promote if an index or publisher will pay" asked the parties who would be displaced to fund their own displacement. If the option beats the incumbent on quality, promote it and fund it to go direct.
- [E2] **When every memo lands on the layer next to your current skills, suspect comfort.** Two eras in a row the org named the frontier (the index, then relevance) and chose the adjacent layer. If the frontier requires a team you lack, then hiring that team *is* the thesis.
- [E2] **Hub-and-spoke networks don't yield a neutral data moat.** When a dominant party (big buyer, carrier, platform) mandates participation, it owns the graph. The spokes are low-volume, won't join a second network, and resent paying. Defensible data accrues to whoever captures *voluntary* intent.
- [E2] **A bubble is a financing event, not validation of the thesis.** When your category becomes the hype, raise or sell into it, hold headcount, and pre-commit kill criteria the bubble can't override. Criteria #2 and #4 fired and were ignored.
- [E2] **Free wins the consumer layer; money pools where intent is expressed and priced.** Mail, browsers and pages went to zero. Queries, auctions and marketplaces got paid.
- [E2 note] E1 "Bridges expire" was CONFIRMED again. Internet EDI transport commoditized on schedule (~2000). Refinement: a bridge priced flat survives as a low-margin utility, not a franchise. E1 "Sell to existing budgets (refined)" was CONFIRMED: EDI budgets funded the wedge and anchored the company to buyer-hub architecture.

## [E3] Lessons from 2002–2007 (Ledgerline, score 58/100)
- [E3] **The auction owner absorbs the measurement.** A platform bundles a tool for free once measurement increases spend on that platform (Google made Analytics free in 2005). It opens APIs rather than banning tools, so it can commoditize them. Neutral tools survive only at the tier that spends across several platforms at scale (enterprise and agencies). Build and price for that tier from day one.
- [E3] **Date your option against the incumbent's ship date, not your own calendar.** If whoever owns the demand side can ship the idea, a long evidence gate is a kill order (AdSense launched before the lift gate). Fund it to launch first, or drop it.
- [E3] **Reserve one thesis slot for what the Playbook can't see.** All three memos optimized on the last era's lesson. The era's new pools (social/UGC, cloud, mobile) were visible only as weak signals in the briefing.
- [E3 note] E2 "Money pools where intent is priced" was CONFIRMED and refined: it pools with whoever *runs the auction*, not with the tools serving bidders. E2 "Suspect comfort" is REVISED: suspect comfort, but don't reject it. Adjacent theses (hosted filtering: Postini/IronPort; hosted apps: Salesforce) both produced large outcomes. Judge on market pull, not identity. E2 "Bubble is a financing event" was CONFIRMED in reverse: bust-era founding and hiring was cheap and well timed. E1 "Bridges expire" held in a new form: the bridge between engines and merchants expired when the engine made it free.

## [E4] Lessons from 2008–2013 (Clearline, score 66/100)
- [E4] **Third-party auctions on an open platform have a 3–4-year window.** Owners let outside networks prove demand, then ship first-party (iAd 2010, AdMob bought 2009–10, Facebook install ads 2012). Plan your exit or your move to the next layer inside that window.
- [E4] **If you can't own the auction, own the referee.** Once platforms run their own auctions, buyers spending across several of them need neutral verification the platforms must accept (mobile measurement partners). This refines the [E3] auction-owner lesson below.
- [E4] **Run the sale on the credit calendar, not the product calendar.** The H1 2008 process beat Lehman by months. A deadline set by the capital climate is worth more than a better price later.
- [E4] **Unanimity right after a reveal is a leakage alarm, and dissents are the control group.** Both E4 dissents (cross-exchange display buyer, cloud analytics) produced comparable real-world outcomes (DSPs, Cloudera/Splunk). Log dissents as tripwires, not as losses.
- [E4] **The reserved slot must go to a curve outside your own data.** In E3 and E4 the "Playbook can't see" slot went to prediction on the company's existing data, both times. The weak signal it listed (the neural-network revival) was the era's deepest curve.
- [E4 note] [E3] "Auction owner absorbs the measurement" is REVISED: it absorbs *single-platform* measurement. Cross-platform arbitration survives and grows as more platforms run auctions. [E2] "Bubble is a financing event" was CONFIRMED in its crash form (sell into the last open window). [E1] "Bridges expire" was CONFIRMED: handset/platform fragmentation shrank to iOS and Android by ~2011. [E3] "Date the option against the incumbent's ship date" was CONFIRMED: launching the exchange in 2008 without a long gate was right.

## [E5] Lessons from 2014–2019 (Clearsight, score 68/100)
- [E5] **A public, benchmarked curve pools value at its scarce inputs** (chips, compute, labeled data, researchers) and with distribution owners. A general API on top is commoditized by hyperscalers within about 2 years (Cloud Vision Dec 2015, Rekognition 2016). Ride a public curve only if you own an input or an outcome loop no giant sees.
- [E5] **Price against the loss, not the labor.** Moderation priced below cents-per-image human queues capped itself. The winners (Forter, Riskified) priced against chargebacks and took the risk.
- [E5] **Don't put the referee on a revenue clock while a platform shift raises the need for it.** Mobile attribution (AppsFlyer, Adjust) became mandatory infrastructure; the Verify sale was the era's clearest own goal.
- [E5] **On a talent-scarce curve, the modal small-company outcome is an acqui-hire.** Plan for it as a valid exit, or build a proprietary data or outcome moat from day one.
- [E5] **Judge a new distribution platform by user behavior, not developer hype.** Western messenger bots (2016) faded within a year; the real messaging platform (WeChat Mini Programs) was closed.
- [E5 note] [E2 note] "certification makes the referee a flat-priced utility" is CONTRADICTED: platform certification became a moat for the leading referees. [E4] "Reserved slot outside your own data" was CONFIRMED: the reserved slot finally went to the era's deepest curve. [E3] "Date the option against the incumbent's ship date" was CONFIRMED: about 18 months passed from the pivot to Google's free SafeSearch API. [E3] "Auction owner absorbs" was CONFIRMED in a new form: Stripe Radar bundled payment fraud scoring. [E4] "Unanimity is a leakage alarm" is still valid: the Convergence Audit is a useful control, but it did not stop the herding.

## [E6] Lessons from 2020–2026 (Clearproof, score 61/100)
- [E6] **When the curve is scale, sell the scarce judgment upstream to whoever trains.** Graded human judgment pooled at labs as training data (Surge ~$1.2B revenue in 2024; Scale; Mercor >$2B ARR in 2026). It pooled far less in enterprise scorecards (evaluation exits ≤ ~$1.7B). Before choosing your buyer, ask "who pays most for this input?"
- [E6] **A tooling layer's market starts when the capability reaches end users, not researchers.** LLM evaluation had almost no buyers from 2020 to 2022, then a crowded field after ChatGPT. Enter early only with burn sized to survive until the adoption event.
- [E6] **Model owners absorb evaluation of their own platforms (OpenAI Evals, Bedrock evaluation; the Humanloop, Statsig and Promptfoo acquisitions). A neutral referee is a franchise only when a third party mandates it** (advertisers, a regulator, a certifier). *(Revises [E4] "own the referee" and [E5] "don't clock the referee": both held because platform certification mandated the referee. Evaluation had no mandate.)*
- [E6] **Outcome pricing wins when you do the work, not when you grade it.** Per-resolution agents (Intercom Fin, Sierra) captured the budget. Graders sold seats. *(Refines [E5] "price against the loss".)*
- [E6] **A self-obsoleting tripwire tied to a measurable artifact is the best decision tool this org has.** It fired within 5 months (GPT-3), and the pivot was correct. Audit it for leakage, though: the more exactly a threshold fits later history, the less it counts as evidence of foresight.
- [E6 note] [E5] "A public curve pools value at scarce inputs" was CONFIRMED at the largest scale in the simulation: Nvidia, the labs, expert data, and power/capex. [E4] "Reserved slot outside your data" was CONFIRMED again (AlphaFold 2 → a Nobel), but 5% as a watch-only slot captured nothing. [E4] "Unanimity is a leakage alarm" held: the board step was written by one hand with no independent Red Team, and leakage scored its worst since E1. [E3] "Date the option against the incumbent's ship date" was CONFIRMED: the model owners took about 3.5 years (Bedrock evaluation, Nov 2023).

## [E7-forecast] Lessons from the live era (Proofworks, forecast score 56/100; no answer key)
- [E7-forecast] **Last era's road not taken is this era's crowded trade.** By Sep 2026, RL environments had 35–40+ vendors, $40–130M rounds (Deeptune, Prime Intellect) and a $1.5B+ license-and-hire deal for Mechanize under discussion. Before adopting a reveal's "missed winner," count the funded entrants and ask what you have that they cannot buy.
- [E7-forecast] **When buyers can build what they buy, sell rights, not labor.** Labs in-source generic environments, run 12+ vendors against each other, and own products (Docs, Claude Code, Codex) that generate verified outcomes for free. The moat test: could the buyer get this through a direct deal with the data owner? If it could, you are a contractor.
- [E7-forecast] **Point the tripwire at the likeliest substitute, not only at the incumbent.** The in-sourcing tripwire watched lab environment generation but missed RL on production deployments and synthetic environment generation, which are the likelier ways the bottleneck dissolves.
- [E7-forecast] **Don't sell the funnel that produces your moat's raw material.** When the thesis depends on rights to real traces, selling the product that collects them (even with carve-outs) is the [E5] "don't clock the referee" error in data form. A carve-out survives only while the acquirer keeps honoring it.

## [E8-forecast] Lessons from the forecast era (Clearwork, forecast score 57/100; no answer key)
- [E8-forecast] **A clearing house is built by the parties who move the money.** DTCC, CLS and the card schemes were founded or co-owned by the incumbents that held the flows. A neutral fee layer next to the rails (AP2, ACP, Visa/Mastercard agent tokens, all FACT(2026)) is a feature request away from absorption. Enter as the rulebook and the data those rails adopt, or bring the flow-holders in as co-owners on day one.
- [E8-forecast] **Stacked speculations multiply.** A thesis resting on five SPEC/EXT premises at 0.4–0.7 each has a joint probability near 10–15%, whatever each memo's confidence says. Count the premises, multiply them, and fund the fallback as if it were the base case.
- [E8-forecast] **Delayed truth is priced by whoever holds the variance.** When outcomes arrive months after payment, the warrantor, carrier or operator absorbing the loss sets the evidence standard; a no-float recorder is a price-taker. *(Refines [E5] "price against the loss" and [E6] "do the work": in a forecast era the org chose the referee again against two dissents that named the loss-holder.)*
- [E8-forecast] **A 24-month kill gate is too slow for a network product.** For settlement and exchange layers, liquidity at month 9–12 (vendors live, repeat settlements, share of volume from the top counterparty) predicts the outcome. Set an early liquidity gate in addition to the scale gate.

## [E9-forecast] Lessons from the final forecast era (Clearbond, forecast score 58/100; no answer key)
- [E9-forecast] **A thesis the grader wrote is not evidence.** When one era's kill case becomes the next era's projected world, and the board then adopts it, the loop is circular. In forecast eras, weight only the FACT layer and independent indicators; treat agreement with the Auditor as the [E4] unanimity alarm.
- [E9-forecast] **The model owner can absorb the warranty, not just the evaluation.** Bundled indemnity (Microsoft's Copilot Copyright Commitment, OpenAI's Copyright Shield, both FACT 2023) is the [E6] absorption pattern applied to risk. Before underwriting a platform's output, ask whether the platform can indemnify it more cheaply, since it holds the traces and ships the updates that reset the loss curve.
- [E9-forecast] **An idea deferred four times arrives crowded.** Guarantees were right in E5 (Forter/Riskified), deferred in E6, E7 and E8, and taken in E9 against 8+ funded entrants and "1–2 dominant modelers". The Comfort Audit's real question is not "is this comfort?" but "was this cheaper to take earlier, when we had fewer competitors and less to lose?" Take the recurring idea small and early with a capped loss, or kill it for good.
