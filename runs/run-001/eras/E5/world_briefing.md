# World Briefing: Era E5 (as of January 1, 2014)
*Prepared by The Record. Only facts public before this date are included.*

## State of computing
- **Mobile:** About 1 billion smartphones shipped in 2013, more than feature phones for the first time. Android runs on roughly four in five new smartphones, from Samsung, many Chinese makers (Xiaomi, Huawei, Lenovo) and others. iOS leads in app spending and in the US and Japan. Microsoft is buying Nokia's phone business; BlackBerry is declining. LTE networks cover most of the US and are spreading in Europe and Asia. PC sales fell about 10% in 2013 as tablets grew.
- **App economy:** Apple's App Store and Google Play each list about a million apps. Free-to-play games dominate top-grossing charts (King's *Candy Crush Saga*; Supercell, in which SoftBank bought a majority stake in October 2013). Apple introduced an advertising identifier (IDFA) in 2012 to replace device IDs. Facebook (app-install ads, 2012) and Twitter (bought MoPub, September 2013) sell mobile app promotion.
- **Cloud:** Amazon Web Services is the clear leader in rented infrastructure, adding data warehousing (Redshift, 2013) and streaming (Kinesis, announced November 2013). Microsoft Azure is growing. Google Compute Engine became generally available in December 2013. Hadoop has commercial vendors (Cloudera, Hortonworks); Berkeley's Spark spun out as Databricks (2013). Docker, released in March 2013, packages applications as portable containers.

## Networks, platforms and commerce
- **Users:** About 2.7 billion people are online, and a growing majority of new users reach the Internet first through a phone.
- **Social and messaging:** Facebook has about 1.2 billion monthly users. It went public in May 2012, and by late 2013 about half of its ad revenue came from mobile. It bought Instagram for about $1B (2012). Twitter went public in November 2013. Messaging apps are growing fast: WhatsApp (about 400 million monthly users, per the company in December 2013), Tencent's WeChat, Line, Kakao and Viber. Snapchat reportedly turned down a $3B offer from Facebook in November 2013.
- **Ads:** Display ads are increasingly bought through real-time auctions. Criteo and Rocket Fuel went public in 2013. Google leads search and mobile ads (AdMob). Mobile measurement companies verify app-install attribution for advertisers.
- **Marketplaces and payments:** Uber (raised $258M from Google Ventures in August 2013), Lyft, Airbnb and delivery startups match people and services through phones. Square and Stripe simplify card payments. Amazon's revenue is above $60B a year. Alibaba is the leading Chinese e-commerce group and is expected to go public.
- **Bitcoin:** The price of the digital currency passed $1,000 in November 2013, then fell after China's central bank barred banks from handling it (December).

## Research frontier
- **Machine learning:** Deep neural networks trained on GPUs have sharply cut error rates in speech recognition (Microsoft, Google, IBM, 2010–12) and in image classification (Krizhevsky, Sutskever and Hinton's ImageNet 2012 entry). Google bought Hinton's startup DNNresearch (March 2013) and published word2vec word embeddings. Facebook opened an AI research lab led by Yann LeCun (December 2013). DeepMind, a London startup, posted a paper in December 2013 on learning to play Atari games from pixels. Voice assistants (Siri 2011, Google Now 2012) and IBM Watson (*Jeopardy!*, 2011) are in market.
- **Vehicles and hardware:** Google has tested self-driving cars on public roads since 2010. Nevada, Florida and California passed laws on autonomous vehicle testing. Wearables and connected devices are early: Google Glass (developer edition), Pebble, Fitbit, Nest thermostats. Oculus shipped a VR developer kit (2013).
- **Life sciences:** Genome sequencing continues to fall in cost. Papers in 2012–13 showed CRISPR-Cas9 editing of genes in human cells. The FDA told 23andMe to stop marketing its health reports (November 2013).
- **Space and energy:** SpaceX supplies the International Space Station under NASA contract and launched a commercial communications satellite to geostationary transfer orbit in December 2013. It is testing a reusable-booster prototype (Grasshopper). Solar panel prices have fallen sharply.

## Capital climate
- The Fed has held rates near zero since December 2008 and is buying bonds. It said in December 2013 it will slow those purchases from January 2014.
- US venture investment is about $30B a year. 2013 was the strongest year for tech IPOs since 2000 (Twitter, Veeva, FireEye). Seed funds and accelerators have multiplied; the JOBS Act (2012) and Kickstarter widened early funding. A November 2013 essay coined "unicorn" for startups valued above $1B (it counted 39).

## Society and regulation
- **Surveillance:** Edward Snowden's leaks (from June 2013) revealed NSA collection programs, including PRISM. That damaged trust in US tech firms abroad and pushed companies to encrypt more traffic. The EU is negotiating a data protection regulation, and the European Parliament voted its version out of committee in October 2013.
- **Other:** A ruling in *Verizon v. FCC* on net-neutrality rules is pending. Cities and regulators are clashing with "sharing economy" services. Mood: very optimistic about mobile and consumer startups; commentators increasingly worry about privacy, inequality and tech valuations.
