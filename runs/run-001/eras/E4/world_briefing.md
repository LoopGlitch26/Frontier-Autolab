# World Briefing: Era E4 (as of January 1, 2008)
*Prepared by The Record. Only facts public before this date are included.*

## State of computing
- **PCs and chips:** Clock speeds have leveled off, and the industry has moved to multicore. Intel's Core 2 (2006) put it back in the lead, and its 45 nm "Penryn" parts shipped in November 2007. Microsoft's **Windows Vista** (January 2007) drew weak reviews and resistance from business users. Nvidia released **CUDA** in 2007 for general-purpose computing on graphics chips.
- **Mobile:** about 3 billion mobile subscriptions worldwide. Nokia leads handsets; RIM's BlackBerry leads in the enterprise; Symbian and Windows Mobile are the main smartphone platforms. Apple launched the **iPhone** in June 2007 (a touchscreen with a full Web browser, on 2G/EDGE, exclusive to AT&T) and said in October that a native software development kit would come in February 2008. Google announced **Android** and the Open Handset Alliance on November 5, 2007, with no devices yet. Amazon shipped the **Kindle** e-reader in November 2007.
- **Infrastructure:** Amazon Web Services sells on-demand storage (S3, March 2006) and compute (EC2, beta since August 2006), and added SimpleDB in December 2007. Google has published papers on MapReduce (2004) and Bigtable (2006), and Amazon on Dynamo (2007). The open-source Hadoop runs at Yahoo. Virtualization (VMware IPO, August 2007) is spreading in data centers.

## Networks and the Web
- **Users:** about 1.3 billion Internet users. About half of US adults have broadband at home.
- **Search and ads:** Google has a majority share of US search and a network of AdSense publishers. It went public in 2004 and reported revenue above $10B for 2006. Yahoo (which bought Overture in 2003) relaunched its ad ranking system ("Panama") in 2007. Microsoft launched adCenter in 2006. In 2007 the display-ad business consolidated: Google agreed to buy **DoubleClick** ($3.1B; FTC cleared it on December 20, EU review pending), Microsoft bought **aQuantive** ($6B), and Yahoo bought **Right Media**. Google Analytics has been free since November 2005.
- **Social and user-generated media:** News Corp bought **MySpace** in 2005. **Facebook** opened to the public in September 2006, launched its developer Platform in May 2007, and in October took a $240M investment from Microsoft at a $15B valuation. Its **Beacon** ad program drew a privacy backlash in November–December 2007. Google bought **YouTube** in 2006 for $1.65B. **Twitter** (2006), Wikipedia, blogs, and Flickr are widely used.
- **Commerce and media:** Amazon, eBay (which owns PayPal and bought Skype in 2005) and Chinese portals are growing. Alibaba.com raised about $1.7B in a Hong Kong IPO in November 2007, and Baidu has been listed since 2005. iTunes dominates legal music downloads, and Netflix began streaming in January 2007.
- **Enterprise software:** software sold by subscription ("SaaS") is established. Salesforce went public in 2004, and NetSuite in December 2007. Hosted email security has been acquired by large firms (Postini by Google, IronPort by Cisco).

## Research frontier
- **Machine learning:** mainstream work uses statistical methods (SVMs, boosting, graphical models, topic models) plus large datasets. The **Netflix Prize** (2006) has made collaborative-filtering research a public competition. Hinton and colleagues' 2006 work on deep belief networks has renewed interest in multilayer neural networks in a few labs. Statistical machine translation (Google Translate) is used in production. Amazon's **Mechanical Turk** (2005) is used to pay people to label data.
- **Robotics:** in the DARPA Urban Challenge (November 2007), autonomous vehicles completed an urban course; Carnegie Mellon's entry won.
- **Life sciences:** DNA sequencing costs are falling fast with next-generation instruments (454, Solexa/Illumina). 23andMe and deCODEme began selling consumer genome scans in November 2007.
- **Other:** clean energy (solar, biofuels) is a major area for venture investment.

## Capital climate
- US venture investment is about $30B a year. IPOs are few, and Sarbanes-Oxley (2002) raised the cost of going public, so acquisitions are the main exit.
- **The credit market is under stress.** US subprime mortgage lenders failed in early 2007 (New Century filed for bankruptcy in April), two Bear Stearns hedge funds collapsed in July, and BNP Paribas froze funds on August 9. Britain's Northern Rock suffered a bank run in September. The Fed cut its policy rate from 5.25% to 4.25% by December and opened a term auction facility for banks. Banks have announced large write-downs. The Dow peaked in October 2007, and oil is near $100 a barrel.

## Society and regulation
- **Privacy:** the FTC held hearings on behavioral advertising and in December 2007 proposed self-regulatory principles. Facebook's Beacon made data sharing a public issue. The EU is reviewing the Google–DoubleClick deal.
- **Networks and IP:** the Associated Press found that Comcast was interfering with BitTorrent traffic, which revived the net-neutrality debate.
- **Climate:** the IPCC's Fourth Assessment Report (2007) raised climate policy on the agenda.
- **Mood:** strong optimism about consumer Web and "Web 2.0," alongside rising worry about credit and housing.
