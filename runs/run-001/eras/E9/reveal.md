## 1. Premise check, and a circularity warning
- **FACT(2026), H:** the ingredients are real today. AIUC writes agent liability on Beazley paper; ISO generative-AI exclusions (CG 40 47/40 48) took effect on 1 Jan 2026; Moffatt v. Air Canada rejected "the AI did it"; AP2/ACP/Visa/Mastercard/Entra define agent mandates. Onchain cover pools such as Nexus Mutual already underwrite software-failure risk at multi-billion scale ([Nexus Mutual](https://nexusmutual.io/blog/no-one-covers-more-onchain-risk-than-nexus-mutual-7-billion-covered)). Programmable per-agent spend limits are already a commodity feature of agent wallets (Openfort, Cobo, MetaMask) ([Openfort](https://www.openfort.io/blog/best-agent-wallets-for-developers), [MetaMask](https://metamask.io/news/agentic-wallet-security)).
- **FACT(2026), H, and against the thesis:** model owners already bundle indemnities: Microsoft's Copilot Copyright Commitment (2023) and OpenAI's Copyright Shield. The owner of the model can absorb the warranty the same way it absorbed evaluation ([E6]).
- **Circularity (the most important finding of this audit):** Victor is right. The E8 Auditor wrote the kill case ("buyers want a warranty, carriers set the evidence standard"), then wrote an E9 world in which human sign-off is gone and disputes resolve as claims. The board then chose the warranty. Agreement with the grader is not evidence. The only independent support for the premise is the 2026 FACT layer above, which shows early demand but no loss data and no winner.
- **Premise count ([E8]):** the board's own ≈18% (safe harbors 0.6 × carrier grants authority and co-owns 0.5 × per-configuration pricing wins 0.6) is honest. Adding the premise it omitted, that model owners do *not* bundle indemnity for enterprise agent work (≈0.6), gives **≈11%** for the wedge working as designed.

## 3. Core-bottleneck confidence and kill case
**Confidence that "nobody can size or transfer loss per agent configuration" is a real, important and separately monetizable bottleneck by end-2045: ~35%.** That agent liability is a large line: ~65% (EXT). That per-configuration pricing beats per-deployer pricing: ~40%. That an independent MGA, not the carrier or the model owner, captures it: ~35% of the remainder.

**Strongest kill case: the model owner indemnifies.** A top-2 lab or platform offers "we pay if our agent breaches its authority" inside the enterprise contract, as Microsoft did for copyright in 2023. It holds the traces, ships the updates that reset the loss curve, and prices risk into the seat. Per-agent bonds become a feature of the platform the way evaluation did ([E6]). Second kill: carriers build per-configuration models on the OTel traces the org itself standardized ([E3]: the auction owner absorbs the measurement).

## 4. Simulated outcome
**Base (~50%):** binding authority with a $30M first-loss slice signed in month 7 (criterion #1 passes one month late). ~140 configurations and ~$35M of bonded authority by month 12: the liquidity gate misses and is extended six months, the first waiver since E6. A 2041 model-update shock at one vendor pushes the loss ratio to ~110% for a quarter; re-rating contains it (#3 does not fire twice). Two labs launch bundled indemnity for their *own* agents in 2042, so Clearbond retreats to open-weight and multi-model deployers, the slowdown-branch market Tomas identified. **2045:** ~$600M gross written premium, ~$180M MGA revenue plus ~$50M data, ~$3–5B value, most likely acquired by the co-owning carrier. Bench closes in 2041 (only 2 co-owning labs). Useful, acquirable, the best-positioned version of the company, still not era-defining.
**Bear (~35%):** no carrier grants first-loss binding authority to a data company; criterion #1 fires, and the company remains a declining data licensor. Or lab indemnities arrive in 2040–41 and cover most target work. Sold 2042–43 for ~$1.5–2.5B.
**Bull (~15%):** a correlated agent-loss event (2041–42) makes per-configuration limits a regulatory requirement for finance safe harbors; Clearbond's re-rating becomes the de facto "agent credit rating." Valued at $10–20B.
