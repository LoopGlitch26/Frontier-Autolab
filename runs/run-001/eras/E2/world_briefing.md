# World Briefing: Era E2 (as of January 1, 1996)
*Prepared by The Record. Only facts public before this date are included.*

## State of computing
- **PCs are entering homes quickly.** Microsoft launched **Windows 95** on August 24, 1995 with a very large marketing campaign. It includes a built-in TCP/IP stack, dial-up networking, the new MSN online service, and (in the Plus! pack) Internet Explorer. Internet Explorer 2.0 shipped in November. Windows NT 3.51 is Microsoft's server and workstation OS. Intel's Pentium (1993) is mainstream, and the Pentium Pro was introduced in November 1995. Typical new PCs ship with 8–16MB RAM, a 500MB–1GB disk, a CD-ROM drive and a 14.4/28.8 kbps modem.
- **Apple is struggling.** It has PowerPC Macs (1994) and has licensed Mac clones (1995), but its market share and margins are falling. Unix workstations (Sun, HP, SGI, DEC) are strong in engineering and in the new Web server market. **Linux** (1991) and the free BSDs are gaining among hobbyists and ISPs.
- **Industry structure:** Microsoft (with its DOJ consent decree of July 1994, and its bid for Intuit abandoned in May 1995 after DOJ objections), Intel, Compaq, IBM (which bought Lotus for about $3.5B in 1995) and Novell (which acquired WordPerfect in 1994) are the major players. Oracle, Sybase, Informix and SAP lead enterprise software. Cisco dominates routers. Lotus Notes is the leading groupware product. Microsoft has announced Exchange Server.
- **Media and devices:** the DVD format was agreed in late 1995. Sony's PlayStation and Sega's Saturn launched in the US in 1995. Digital cellular (GSM in Europe, digital standards and PCS spectrum auctions in the US) is expanding. Pen computers and PDAs (Newton, GO) have disappointed.

## Networks and the Web
- **The Internet is now commercial.** The NSFNET backbone was retired on April 30, 1995, and traffic runs over commercial backbones interconnected at exchange points. Hosts number several million and are growing roughly 70–100% per year. Commercial ISPs include UUNET, PSINet, Netcom, BBN, MCI and Sprint. Network Solutions began charging for .com domain registration in September 1995.
- **The World Wide Web** (CERN, 1991) has grown fast since NCSA Mosaic (1993). Web sites number in the tens of thousands and are multiplying every few months. **Netscape** (founded April 1994) holds most of the browser market with Navigator and went public in August 1995 at a first-day valuation near $2B+. Spyglass licenses Mosaic, including to Microsoft. Microsoft outlined a broad Internet strategy on December 7, 1995.
- **Finding things:** directories and search engines include Yahoo (incorporated in 1995 with Sequoia backing), Lycos, Excite, Infoseek, WebCrawler, and DEC's AltaVista (launched December 1995 with a very large full-text index).
- **New platforms and services:** Sun announced **Java** in May 1995, and Netscape licensed it. SSL secures Web transactions. RealAudio (1995) streams sound, and VocalTec sells Internet telephony software. Early commerce includes Amazon.com (opened July 1995), AuctionWeb (September 1995), CyberCash, DigiCash, First Virtual and Open Market. Visa and MasterCard are working on specifications for card payments online.
- **Online services:** AOL (about 4–5M members) and CompuServe (about 4M) lead, with Prodigy and MSN behind them. All are adding Web access. Most home users connect by dial-up modem. ISDN is available but slow to spread, and cable-modem trials are underway.
- **Email:** SMTP/MIME is the de facto standard between organizations. X.400 and X.500 have stalled, and LDAP (1993) is the lightweight directory protocol.

## Research frontier
- **AI and learning:** neural networks, HMMs and statistical methods are established in speech and handwriting research. Support-vector machines (Cortes & Vapnik, 1995), reinforcement learning (TD-Gammon), and "data mining / knowledge discovery" (the KDD conferences) are active areas. CMU's Navlab drove "No Hands Across America" in 1995. IBM's Deep Blue team is preparing to play Kasparov in early 1996. Continuous dictation is not yet commercial.
- **Other:** Shor's 1994 factoring algorithm has sparked interest in quantum computing, and ion-trap gate proposals appeared in 1995. The first complete bacterial genome (*H. influenzae*) was sequenced in 1995, and the Human Genome Project is ongoing.

## Capital climate
- **US equities are strong:** the Nasdaq passed 1,000 in 1995, and the Dow ended the year above 5,000. Internet IPOs (Netscape, Spyglass, UUNET, PSINet, Netcom) have drawn intense investor interest, and venture fundraising is recovering. The Fed raised rates through 1994 and cut them in July and December 1995 (fed funds 5.5%).
- **Abroad:** Japan is in a post-bubble slump.

## Society and regulation
- **Telecom reform:** a major telecom reform bill, which includes the Communications Decency Act provisions on online content, is near final passage in Congress.
- **Security and privacy:** encryption export controls remain, and the 1993 Clipper chip proposal was widely opposed. Online pornography and hackers are frequent media themes. The EU adopted its Data Protection Directive in October 1995.
- **Trade:** the WTO began operating on January 1, 1995.
- **Public attitude:** the "information superhighway" is the public buzzword. The Internet is widely seen as the next mass medium, but its business models are unproven.
