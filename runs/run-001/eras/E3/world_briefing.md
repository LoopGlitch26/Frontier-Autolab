# World Briefing: Era E3 (as of January 1, 2002)
*Prepared by The Record. Only facts public before this date are included.*

## State of computing
- **PCs:** Microsoft shipped **Windows XP** on October 25, 2001, merging the consumer and NT lines and bundling Messenger and Passport sign-in. Analysts report that 2001 PC shipments fell for the first time in about 15 years. The Pentium 4 passed 2 GHz, and 64-bit Itanium shipped. **HP announced a merger with Compaq** in September 2001, which Hewlett family heirs are contesting.
- **Apple** shipped Mac OS X in March 2001, opened retail stores and launched the **iPod** (a 5 GB music player) in October.
- **Servers and software:** Linux is established on servers. IBM pledged about $1B to it, and Red Hat went public in 1999. Enterprise development is split between Java/J2EE (IBM, BEA, Sun) and Microsoft's **.NET** (announced June 2000). "Web services" (XML, SOAP, WSDL, UDDI) is the integration theme.
- **Microsoft antitrust:** the 2000 breakup order was overturned in June 2001. The DOJ reached a settlement in November, and some states are still litigating. Xbox launched in November 2001.

## Networks and the Web
- **Users:** about 500M worldwide. Roughly 10M+ US households have broadband (cable and DSL), and most still dial up. Excite@Home went bankrupt in September 2001.
- **Wireless:** 802.11b ("Wi-Fi") and Bluetooth are shipping. NTT DoCoMo's **i-mode** has tens of millions of subscribers, and DoCoMo launched 3G in October 2001. European carriers paid more than $100B for 3G spectrum in 2000 and are heavily indebted. BlackBerry is popular with corporate users.
- **Search and portals:** Yahoo, AOL and MSN dominate portal traffic. **Google** has supplied Yahoo's search results since mid-2000, claims more than 1B indexed pages and sells text ads (AdWords, 2000). **Overture** (formerly GoTo.com) syndicates pay-per-click listings to major portals. Ask Jeeves owns Direct Hit. Online ad spending fell sharply in 2001.
- **Media:** **AOL Time Warner** closed its merger in January 2001. **Napster** was shut down in July 2001, and decentralized file sharing (Gnutella, Kazaa) is growing. Wikipedia (January 2001), Blogger and LiveJournal show users producing content.
- **Commerce:** Amazon and eBay are the leading survivors. **PayPal** filed for an IPO in autumn 2001. Webvan, eToys and Pets.com have failed. The E-SIGN Act (2000) validates electronic signatures.
- **B2B:** most public exchanges have closed. Ariba, Commerce One and VerticalNet are down more than 90%, and Covisint and industry consortia continue. EDIINT (Internet EDI), RosettaNet and ebXML 1.0 are the standards in play.
- **Security:** the Code Red and Nimda worms (2001) exposed server and email weaknesses, and spam is rising.

## Research frontier
- **Machine learning:** SVMs, boosting (including Viola–Jones real-time face detection, 2001), random forests and graphical models dominate. Latent Dirichlet Allocation appeared at NIPS 2001. Statistical methods lead machine translation and speech. Neural networks are unfashionable outside a few groups. Link analysis (PageRank, HITS) is standard in Web retrieval.
- **Other:** chips are at the 130 nm process node. Grid and volunteer computing (Globus, SETI@home) are active, and IBM has an "autonomic computing" agenda. In December 2001 IBM factored 15 on a 7-qubit NMR quantum computer. Draft human genomes were published in February 2001.

## Capital climate
- The Nasdaq peaked at 5,048 in March 2000 and closed 2001 near 1,950. The US entered recession in March 2001, and the Fed cut rates 11 times, to 1.75%. Venture investment has fallen by more than half from 2000, the IPO window is nearly shut, and down rounds are common. Telecom is overbuilt. Exodus failed, and **Enron** filed for bankruptcy on December 2, 2001, raising questions about accounting.
- **Global:** **China joined the WTO** on December 11, 2001. Euro cash circulates from January 1, 2002. Indian IT-services firms are growing on offshore outsourcing.

## Society and regulation
- The **September 11** attacks were followed by the **USA PATRIOT Act** (October 2001) and rising security spending.
- **Privacy and IP:** the DMCA, COPPA, the DoubleClick/Abacus controversy and the US–EU Safe Harbor (2000) define the landscape. Encryption export controls were largely relaxed in 2000.
- **Mood:** deep skepticism about the "new economy," while Internet use keeps growing.
