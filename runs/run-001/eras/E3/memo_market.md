# Market & Capital: Department Memo, Era E3 (January 2002)

*Attendees: Ortiz, Mehta, Laurent. Sources: E3 briefing, E2 decision and reveal, Playbook. Nothing after Jan 1, 2002.*

## Transcript

**Carmen Ortiz, Head of GTM:** Our 5,000 suppliers pay us because a buyer makes them, and they tell me so on every renewal call. But ask what they'd pay for and it's the same answer: "send me a customer who isn't the one squeezing me." In a recession, new orders survive; compliance doesn't.

**Arjun Mehta, CFO:** We have ~$15M, a preference stack from a peak-era round, and no IPO window. Any new round now is a punishing down round, so the next thesis has to be funded from our own cash and the EDI utility's margin. The trough also works for us. Search talent and dead exchanges' catalogs are cheap. Per [E2] bubble lesson inverted: a crash is a buying event, not a verdict.

**Dr. Elise Laurent, Chief Economist:** The margin map is plain. Display ads collapsed in 2001, but Overture and Google are selling pay-per-click listings at rising prices. Transport, including our own, is a commodity. Money pools where a buyer states intent and a seller bids for it [E2]. The largest unpriced intent left is *commercial*: a purchasing manager looking for a new supplier. Today it goes to print directories and trade shows. China's WTO entry will multiply it, because every Western buyer is now asking "who can make this cheaper, and can I trust them?"

**Carmen:** Then our flat supplier fee is the wrong price. A machine shop will pay more per qualified inquiry than it ever paid for EDI.

**Arjun:** Google or Yahoo could add a "business" tab and take it all.

**Elise:** They rank pages, not suppliers, and can't see who ships on time. Our receipted orders are a trust signal no crawler has, *if* buyers choose to come.

**Arjun:** My preference is the money, not the leads. Buyers are stretching payment terms, and banks are pulling back from small borrowers. Our buyer-acknowledged invoices are nearly collateral, and a partner bank funds early payment.

**Elise:** That's the adjacent-skill trap [E2] again, and the buyer still owns that graph [E2 hub-and-spoke]. I'd keep it as the second layer.

## DEPARTMENT RECOMMENDATION

### Thesis A (preferred): The priced intent layer for business sourcing
- **Chain:** Pay-per-click auctions and link-analysis search work at scale → procurement staff start searching the open Web for new and offshore suppliers → there's no trusted, searchable index of *supplier capability and performance*; general engines rank pages, and print directories are static → **we own a verified supplier index plus a pay-per-inquiry auction, ranked partly on receipted delivery history** → data: buyer queries and RFQs (voluntary intent) joined to fulfillment outcomes → next capability: supplier performance scoring, then trade assurance/escrow on cross-border orders.
- **Why now:** the crash left CPCs low and procurement teams short-staffed. China's WTO accession creates a flood of new sourcing searches.
- **Wedge:** free verified profiles for our 5,000 suppliers plus defunct-exchange catalogs bought cheaply. Buyers search free. Suppliers pay per qualified inquiry. We also syndicate paid listings into the major engines' commercial queries.
- **First customer:** a US contract manufacturer or packaging supplier already on our network, seeking non-mandated buyers.
- **Makes obsolete:** trade directories, trade-show lead lists and sourcing-agent fees. Also **our own mandate-priced Web-EDI**: we stop charging for compliance and charge for demand.

### Thesis B (option): Receipted working capital for small suppliers
- **Chain:** Signed receipts plus E-SIGN make buyer-approved invoices legally usable → big buyers stretch terms in the recession → suppliers are starved of cash and small-business lending is contracting → **we own the approved-invoice platform and early-payment marketplace (banks fund it, we price it)** → data: buyer payment behavior and supplier delivery performance → next capability: automated small-business trade credit scoring.
- **Why now:** rates at 1.75%; post-Enron, "verified transaction" sells.
- **Wedge:** an early-payment program for one anchor buyer's supplier tail.
- **First customer:** an anchor retailer that wants 60-day terms without breaking its suppliers, paired with a regional bank.
- **Makes obsolete:** traditional factoring and paper invoice verification. It makes EDI a free feature.

### Capital plan
Run the EDI utility to breakeven as a saleable asset ([E1] bridges expire). Put ≥40% of burn on Thesis A with a hired search/ranking team (per [E2] comfort lesson, the hire is the thesis). No priced round until inquiry revenue shows traction. Thesis B goes to a bank-partner pilot at ≤10% of burn.

### Playbook lessons applied
- **[E2] Money pools where intent is expressed and priced:** this drives Thesis A.
- **[E2] Hub-and-spoke:** this is why A rather than B is primary. Buyer queries are voluntary, and mandated invoices are not.
- **[E2] Tripwire gate:** B is promoted on default rate and funded volume, not bank enthusiasm.
- **[E2] Comfort lesson:** argued *partly against* ourselves. A reuses our supplier base, which is comfortable, but it demands a ranking team we lack.
- **[E1] Pipe, then traffic, then index:** the business Web is now large enough for a vertical index to pay.

### Most uncertain
1. Whether procurement intent moves to open search, or stays with directories, trade shows and relationships (our biggest unknown).
2. Whether general engines or the existing Asian export portals (Alibaba, Global Sources) own this intent before a US vertical can.
3. Whether data from 20 hubs is broad enough to be a real trust signal.
