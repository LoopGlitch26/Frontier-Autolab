## 2. The Playbook, distilled (the durable 10)
1. **Bridges expire when the standards war ends.** Confirmed E1–E4; the most reliable lesson.
2. **The platform owner absorbs the tool.** E3 (free Analytics), E4 (first-party install ads), E5 (Cloud Vision, Radar), E6 (model-owner evals), E7 (lab in-sourcing); extended in E9 to warranties.
3. **A referee is a franchise only when a third party mandates it.** Revised from E4 and E5 to the E6 mandate condition; it matters because the org's signature asset was always a referee.
4. **Money pools where intent or loss is priced, with whoever holds it.** Refined in E2, E3, E5, E6 and E8; never contradicted.
5. **A public curve pools value at its scarce inputs.** Confirmed E5 and E6 at the largest scale in the simulation (chips, labs, expert data).
6. **Sell on the credit calendar.** Confirmed E4 (before Lehman) and E5 (the 2014 mobile window). These were the best-executed moves in the run.
7. **Date an option against the incumbent's ship date.** Confirmed E3, E4, E5 and E6. Long evidence gates are kill orders.
8. **Hub-and-spoke networks don't yield a data moat; voluntary intent does.** Confirmed E2 and reused as a design rule through E8.
9. **Last era's road not taken is this era's crowded trade.** Set in E7 and confirmed in E9, when the guarantee was taken against 8+ entrants.
10. **Stacked speculations multiply; fund the fallback as the base case.** Set in E8 and applied honestly in E9 (~11–18%).

**Revised:** "suspect comfort" became "judge it on market pull" (E3). "Certification makes the referee a utility" was contradicted (E5). Tripwires must be gated on capability, not the incumbent's wallet (E2).

## 3. Meta-lesson: what predicted winners, and what fooled the org

**Named the frontier, picked the adjacent layer.** This is the defining failure, and it repeated in every era. E1 named hypertext and the index, then built a gateway. E2 named relevance, then built EDI. E3 named contextual matching and had hosted apps in its dissents, then built bid tools. E4 flagged neural networks and funded none. E5 took the right curve (deep learning) but sold a general API on it. E6 saw scale and sold evaluation instead of upstream judgment. E8 named the loss-holder and built a clearing fee. The memos found the frontier; the board's filter, "what can our assets reach in 18 months," moved each call one layer toward comfort.

**The recurring deferred guarantee.** It appeared in E5 as the phase-3 step, and in that era Forter and Riskified won with exactly this. It was deferred to end-2022 in E6, gated in E7, made the "next capability" in E8, and taken in E9. This was the org's most persistent correct instinct, executed at the most crowded moment. Its real blocker was never the idea. It was the refusal to hold any risk on the balance sheet, a rule that protected the org and also kept it out of the layer where margin pooled.

**The reserved-slot curve.** In E3 and E4 the slot went to comfort (prediction on the company's own data) while neural networks went unfunded. From E5 the slot pointed at the right curve (protein structure in E6, embodied agents in E7, verification in E9) at 5–15% weight. In-sim it never paid off once. Lesson: a correctly aimed reserved slot at 10% is a research budget, not a hedge.

**Dissents were right more often than the decisions:** neural nets (E1), web traffic (E2), hosted apps and B2B sourcing (E3), DSPs and cloud analytics (E4), the referee (E5), all three in E6, the funnel (E7), the loss-holder (E8). Dissents naming *who holds the money or loss* or *who will absorb us* were right almost every time; dissents naming a technology curve were right on science, wrong on timing.

**Predicted winners:** the usable layer of a winning protocol, the auction owner, scarce inputs, mandated referees, doing the work, exits timed to credit. **Fooled the org:** bridges, neutral tools on rented land, general APIs, hub networks, unmandated referees, and (from E4) answering the previous scorecard.

## 4. The trained forecast
- **2026: agent evidence with data rights, not environments as labor.** Proofworks, with the audit's correction applied: the durable asset is rights to real deployment traces and loss outcomes, not environment labor. Confidence the bottleneck is real by 2031: **~35%**.
- **2032: consequence data and acceptance standards for delegated agent work.** Clearwork's fallback, not its settlement wedge. Networks and labs absorb settlement. Confidence: **~30%** that this is separately monetizable.
- **2040: per-agent authority limits and loss pricing.** This is Clearbond. Confidence: **~35%**, discounted further because the grader wrote the world it rests on.

The through-line from E5 to E9 is stable: **value migrates from recording what agents did to carrying the loss when they fail.**

## 6. Honest limitations
- **Hindsight:** the agents are an LLM that knows 1990–2020. Leakage scored 5 (E4) and 4 (E6), and E4–E6 pivots track the prior reveals.
- **E6's board step was written by the orchestrator**, with no independent Red Team.
- **Forecast eras have no ground truth.** The E8 kill case became the E9 world, which the E9 board adopted: circular.
- **One model plays all 16 agents, The Record and The Auditor.** Dissent is simulated diversity, and the grader shares the judge's priors.
- **Outcomes are invented distributions** compounding across eras.
